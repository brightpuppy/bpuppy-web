The situation

Most buildings already have pet services. They just have no control over them.

Residents hire walkers privately. Each one hires someone different, and the building ends up letting people it has never met come and go with keys to occupied apartments.

Today

Nobody knows who is in the building

A different walker every few months, arranged by text message. The front desk has no list and no way to check one against the other.

Today

No insurance behind the door

If a dog bites someone in the lobby or a walker floods a unit, there is often no policy to point at, and the question lands on the board.

Today

Nothing is written down

When something goes wrong there is no record of who was there, at what time, or what happened. The super remembers, or nobody does.

The service

Three services, the same people every week.

Staff are assigned to your building, not rotated through it. The front desk gets the names and the schedule in advance.

  • Dog walking 30 min · 12 h notice

    Scheduled walks with a written start and end time.

  • Vet transport 90 min · 24 h notice

    Pick-up, the appointment, and back home. The resident does not have to leave work.

  • Grooming 120 min · 24 h notice

    Full groom, booked through the same account.

What the board gets

The paperwork a board asks for, before it asks.

  • Certificate of insuranceGeneral liability and care-custody-control, with the corporation and the managing agent named as additional insured. Renewals sent automatically.
  • W-9 and signed agreementExecuted electronically. Both stay downloadable from your board portal for as long as the agreement is active.
  • Named staffWho is assigned to your building, with the schedule. Changes reach the front desk before they happen, not after.

We do not contact a single resident until the board tells us to.

Confidentiality

The board sees the building. It never sees the residents.

This is not a promise about our intentions. It is how the system is built: the board portal cannot retrieve resident records, because the queries that would return them do not exist on that side.

The board sees

  • How many residents are enrolled
  • How many services ran this month
  • The status of the agreement and the insurance
  • Incidents in common areas, written for the board
  • Who from our staff is assigned to the building

The board never sees

  • Which residents are enrolled, or their apartments
  • What anyone pays, or whether they are behind
  • Any pet's medical records, medication or vet
  • The internal write-up of an incident
  • Anything at all about another building

Getting started

Four steps. The building signs nothing on the first call.

  1. You askSend the form below. We reply within one business day with the service outline, the certificate of insurance and a W-9, so the board has the documents in hand before it decides anything.
  2. The board reviewsUsually one meeting. We can join it or stay out of it, whichever you prefer. Questions about liability get answered in writing.
  3. Agreement signedElectronically, by whoever the board designates. The corporation is named on the policy the same week.
  4. Residents enrollThe building gets its own sign-up page. We do not contact residents before this point, and we never solicit anyone the board has not approved us to reach.
